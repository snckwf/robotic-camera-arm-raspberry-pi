LCD display for the camera arm

A 16×2 LCD display showing the joint positions, the light and Claude's messages. It is mounted on the lid of the box, fixed or swivelling, or away from the arm.

Printed parts

Item 13 of the parts list: display box in two pieces (shell with the inclined window and bottom with the tab). PETG, no supports.

Hardware and components

Display

  • 16x2 HD44780 LCD + PCF8574 I2C module — 1
  • BSS138 4-channel I2C level shifter — 1
  • 4 wires (any length, about 1 m max)

Screws

  • M3×6 — 4 (LCD in the shell)
  • M3×8 — 4 (bottom)
  • M3 with nut — 2 (fixing on the lid) or 1 with washer (swivelling)

Wiring

  • GPIO2 (SDA) / GPIO3 (SCL) → level shifter LV1 / LV2 (on the same terminals as the PCA9685)
  • 3V3 → level shifter LV, only if the module does not already have a regulator (see test in step 1)
  • +5V terminals → level shifter HV and VCC of the LCD I2C module
  • GND terminals → level shifter GND and LCD GND
  • Level shifter HV1 / HV2 → SDA / SCL of the LCD I2C module

Steps

STEP 01 / 3

BSS138 level shifter

BSS138 4-channel level shifterDupont or solder wires
  1. Solder the pin strips to the module and push it (pins up) into its seat in the electronics box, next to the PCA9685, until it clicks under the small teeth.
  2. Test it before connecting it to the Pi: power only its 5 V side (HV and GND) and measure the LV pin with the tester. If you already read about 3.3 V the module has an onboard regulator: the 3V3 wire from the Pi must not be connected.
  3. Connect the Pi side: LV1 → GPIO2 (SDA) and LV2 → GPIO3 (SCL) on the breakout, together with the PCA9685 wires (the I2C bus is shared); LV → 3V3 (only if the module has no regulator); GND → GND terminals.
  4. Connect the 5 V side: HV → +5V terminals. HV1 and HV2 are the outputs for the display SDA and SCL.
With the power disconnected: never swap the HV side and the LV side. The diagram is above, under «Wiring».
STEP 02 / 3

Display box

  1. Solder the I2C module (PCF8574) behind the LCD and solder 4 wires (GND, VCC, SDA, SCL) to its pins, or use Dupont jumpers.
  2. Screw the LCD inside the shell with 4 M3×6 screws into the bosses behind the window: the metal frame goes into the window and sits flush.
  3. Route the 4 wires out through the hole in the bottom.
  4. Close with the bottom: 4 M3×8 screws from below, heads recessed.
  5. Take the 4 wires to the electronics box: VCC and GND to the terminals, SDA and SCL to the HV1 and HV2 pins of the level shifter (see step 1).
With the display on, set the contrast with the blue trimmer of the I2C module before closing the bottom. First measure the height of the metal frame (lcd_corn, 7 mm) and the hole spacing (lcd_fori).
STEP 03 / 3

Where to mount it

2 M3 screws (fixed)1 M3 screw (swivelling)
  1. On the box lid, fixed: mark the 2 tab holes where it is most comfortable to read, drill the lid with a 3 mm bit and screw it down with 2 M3 screws and nuts. The wires go in through the round hole of the lid.
  2. On the lid, adjustable: drill a single hole and fix it with a single M3 screw with washer, tightened just enough. This way you can turn it slightly towards you when you move around the bench.
  3. Remote, anywhere on the bench: extend the 4 wires (5 V, GND, SDA, SCL from the HV side). Keep them short, about 1 m at most, better if twisted in pairs.
  4. Power on: the display shows «Camera arm» and then the joint positions. Adjust the contrast with the blue trimmer if you cannot read anything.
Before drilling the lid, check that there are no boards or wires under the chosen spot.

Software

  • Enable I2C on the Raspberry (sudo raspi-config → Interface Options → I2C) and install python3-smbus2.
  • Copy display_lcd.py to /opt/braccio/ and display-lcd.service to /etc/systemd/system/, then sudo systemctl enable --now display-lcd.
  • Test: i2cdetect -y 1 must show 40 (PCA9685) and 27 or 3f (display).
  • Claude writes on it with the «scrivi_display» (write display) tool; the controller, if present, shows STOP, HOME and light on it.
